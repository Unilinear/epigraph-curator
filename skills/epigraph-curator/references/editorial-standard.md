# Editorial Standard

## Domain language

**Target Document** — the Markdown file being framed.

**Framing Intent** — the proposition or design value the epigraph should make the reader feel before entering the prose. It is not a summary or keyword list.

**Source Quotation** — wording found in a traceable source and conventionally associated with a named author. It is not wording supplied by model memory.

**Semantic Invariant** — the Source Quotation’s core proposition. It must survive excerpting or translation.

**Lead Epigraph** — the one candidate selected after all gates pass.

**Editorial Refusal** — a deliberate no-answer when quality or evidence is inadequate.

## The three gates

### Rhetorical Fit

The epigraph should change how the reader enters this particular document. It may approach indirectly, but the relationship must be explainable in one specific sentence.

Reject a candidate when it:

- repeats a topic word without framing the argument;
- conflicts with the document’s actual stance;
- needs a paragraph of explanation to seem relevant; or
- contributes only the fame of its author.

### Distinctiveness

Perform the substitution test: could this line introduce several unrelated projects just as naturally? If yes, it is generic.

Reject default motivational staples, universal claims about success, and lines selected chiefly because they are recognizable.

### Elegance

Prefer compression, tension, image, and restraint. The card should be visually and intellectually subordinate to the document.

Reject:

- exhortation and poster wisdom;
- ornamental profundity;
- inflated diction;
- jokes that collapse after one reading;
- excerpts requiring so much context that they cease to stand alone.

## Selection rules

- Gates are pass/fail, not a numerical score.
- Authority and recognizability are tie-breakers only.
- Cultural and linguistic variety should emerge from fit, not tokenism.
- A controversial author is not automatically disqualified, but avoid a choice whose public meaning overwhelms the Target Document unless that tension is intentional.
- Existing epigraphs have a status-quo advantage: replace one only for a material improvement.
- One strong answer is the default. A shortlist is a user-requested exception.

## Refusal rules

Refuse when:

- no candidate passes every gate;
- the user’s constraints force a weak or unverifiable result;
- only model memory or quote-aggregation pages support the wording;
- a safe, faithful rendering cannot be made; or
- the Target Document has no stable Framing Intent.

A refusal should identify the blocking gate or evidence problem, not manufacture a consolation quote.
