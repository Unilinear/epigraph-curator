# Source and Attribution Policy

Trust is part of the product. A beautiful misquotation fails.

## Candidate record

Before selecting a Source Quotation, retain:

- exact source-language wording;
- source language;
- conventional author;
- work or object title;
- stable section, page, line, chapter, plate, manuscript, or object locator;
- direct URL;
- evidence class;
- Semantic Invariant;
- intended rendering mode: exact, excerpt, translation, or adaptation;
- a brief rights-risk note when the source may still be copyrighted.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. a primary text, manuscript, object record, or scan held by the author/publisher, a library, museum, archive, or university;
2. a scholarly or critical edition with a stable locator;
3. a reputable institutional transcription that identifies its witness.

A quote site, social post, search snippet, unattributed image, or another model’s answer may suggest a lead but can never establish wording or attribution. Open and inspect the underlying source.

When sources disagree, prefer the strongest witness and preserve historically meaningful spelling. If the difference cannot be resolved compactly, choose another candidate or refuse.

## Rendering modes

### Exact

Preserve wording. Silent changes are limited to typography that does not alter language, such as converting straight quotation marks to the document’s style. Record any normalization in the hidden note.

Direct attribution is allowed:

```md
— Author
```

### Excerpt

Keep the original order and proposition. Mark omissions with an ellipsis and editorial insertions with brackets. Do not splice distant fragments into a sentence the author never wrote.

Direct attribution is allowed when the excerpt remains faithful.

### Translation

Show the exact source-language wording first and the document-language rendering second. Do not prefix the second line with visible prose such as `Translation:`; the fixed order keeps the card quiet. Translate the proposition faithfully and do not improve the author’s claim to fit the document.

Attribute the original to the author. Identify the translation rendering on the preview’s provenance line outside the card, and record `translation: Epigraph Curator rendering` in the hidden note unless a named, licensed translation was actually used.

### Adaptation

Adaptation means wording changed beyond a faithful translation or excerpt. It is not the default and must preserve the Semantic Invariant.

Use:

```md
— After Author
```

Never place adapted wording above direct `— Author` attribution. Explain the change in the hidden note.

## Copyright restraint

Prefer public-domain originals. For material that may still be copyrighted:

- use only the brief passage necessary for commentary and framing;
- never reproduce a complete modern poem, song lyric, paywalled passage, or substantial excerpt;
- do not use pirate or provenance-free copies;
- choose a public-domain alternative when rights or provenance are unclear.

This policy is editorial caution, not legal advice.

## Offline behavior

Without Web access, the bundled catalog is the complete admissible source set. Its URLs provide provenance but do not require a live request. If the catalog cannot supply a distinctive fit, issue an Editorial Refusal. Never fill the gap from model memory.
